CASE STUDY — DTC SUPPLEMENTS

The Purest Co.

Competitor research and a proposed creative concept, built around one question: what does it actually take to earn trust in a crowded supplement category?

Objective

Conduct competitor research to identify strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities across positioning, messaging, creative, and customer journey.

Key Findings

01

Social Proof

Strongest around established hero products, while newer SKUs have considerably less review volume.

02

Transparency

A major positioning pillar, reinforced through the brand's Purest Promise, PurestLabs, and lab-testing information.

03

Customer Journey

Incorporates guided selling, subscriptions, and loyalty mechanisms to move customers toward purchase and repeat purchasing.

Customer Insight

The customer isn't simply looking for a supplement — they need a reason to trust one.

Purest repeatedly addresses skepticism through transparency, testimonials, product demonstrations, and testing information.

Strategic Opportunity

Skepticism can be treated as a creative opportunity rather than something to avoid. Instead of immediately telling customers "our product works," the creative can acknowledge their skepticism and let the customer's own journey and available evidence build trust.

Creative Concept: "The Skeptic"

Format: UGC  ·  Objective: Turn skepticism into the hook
  1. Hook: "I know this probably isn't going to work... but I'm trying it anyway."
  2. Set Up: The creator explains why they're skeptical and decides to try the product.
  3. Journey: Show product usage, daily routine, and progress.
  4. Payoff: "Okay... I actually didn't expect this."
  5. Proof: Genuine customer experiences, testimonials, or substantiated product evidence.
  6. CTA: "See what Purest is about."

Execution

Scene 01 — Problem Recognition

Four need-states, one question

"Looking for support with menopause, clearer-looking skin, breakouts, or bloating?"
Introduce four different customers, each representing one need-state.
Purpose: make the viewer recognize their own problem before the product ever appears.
Scene 02 — Product Relevance

The product enters — briefly

"The Purest Co. created targeted formulas designed to support each of these needs."
Each person holds the relevant product; the line briefly communicates what it's designed to support before moving to evidence.
Scene 03 — Proof

Evidence, not claims

"Don't just take our word for it. See what customers experienced for themselves."
Customer testimonials, product demonstrations, reviews, and testing information.
Purpose: directly address the trust barrier identified in research.
Scene 04 — Offer

Present the verified offer

₱5,100₱4,300
"Start your 30-day routine today."

Why This Creative Could Work

Problem before product

Most supplement advertising opens with the product. This concept opens with the customer's problem, so the viewer identifies with the situation first.

Addresses skepticism directly

The creative acknowledges skepticism instead of pretending it doesn't exist, then answers it with testimonials and evidence.
