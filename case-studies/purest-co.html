CASE STUDY

The Purest Co.

DTC Supplements — Competitor Research & Creative Concept

Independent research project exploring trust vs. skepticism in the supplements category, plus a full proposed UGC concept.

The problem I looked at

The Purest Co. sells clean, science-backed supplements. In a category full of hype and distrust, their biggest challenge is convincing skeptical buyers that the product is worth trying.

I treated this like a real client account: researched their positioning, customer language, and existing creative, then built a concept designed to attack the trust gap head-on.

Ad analysis

I broke down one of their Meta/UGC-style ads. The structure moved from a relatable problem → social proof → product introduction. It worked, but it stayed surface-level on the skepticism many first-time buyers feel.

Key takeaway for supplement brands: leading with "I was skeptical too" often outperforms pure benefit claims because it mirrors the actual customer mindset.

Proposed concept: "The Skeptic"

A UGC-style concept that starts with distrust and walks the viewer through the exact mental journey a cautious buyer goes through.

Scene-by-scene

1. Hook — Creator looking at camera: "I almost didn't buy this. Here's why."

2. The doubt — Lists the usual red flags (too many claims, unknown brand, price).

3. The test — What they actually checked (ingredients, third-party testing, reviews from people like them).

4. The result — Honest outcome after 2–3 weeks.

5. Soft CTA — "If you're still on the fence, at least check the label."

This concept is designed to be tested directly against their current creative. The angle is pure trust-building instead of pure benefit.

What this shows

I can take a real brand, dig into the customer tension, and turn it into a concrete creative concept with a clear testing hypothesis — the same process I would use for a paying client.
