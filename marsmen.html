CASE STUDY — DTC MEN'S HEALTH & WELLNESS

MarsMen

Research MarsMen's existing marketing, identify gaps and opportunities, and develop a performance-focused creative concept from those findings.

Research Findings

OBSERVATION 01

Dosing Could Create Friction

MarsMen's recommended serving requires multiple capsules. For someone unfamiliar with the product, that could raise real questions: why so many capsules, how they should be taken, and how it fits into a daily routine.

Strategic opportunity: make the routine simple and transparent rather than leaving the customer to figure it out.

Creative Concept: "I Want My Drive Back"

Format: Paid UGC / Creator-Led Performance Ad

An athlete realizes the drive and motivation he once had doesn't feel the same anymore. He tries to improve his routine, and eventually — in a moment of frustration — discovers MarsMen and incorporates it into his routine.

Creative Execution

Scene 01 — Problem Recognition

The energy is intentionally low

"I started thinking my age was finally catching up with me. I used to train because I wanted to win. Now I just wanted to finish and go home."
The athlete trains, but slower — minimal expression, muted tone. He narrates over his own training footage.
MarsMen already leans on athletes for identity. Instead of removing that, the athlete introduces the customer's problem before the product is even named.
Scene 02 — Trying To Fix It

Doing the right things isn't enough

"So I tried fixing everything I could. And yeah, it helped. But I didn't want to just maintain. I wanted the same drive I had when I first started."
Better sleep, cleaner nutrition, consistent training — his existing routine, maintained.
Showing what he already tried makes the story believable, and opens the gap between "I'm doing the right things" and "I still don't feel like myself."
Scene 03 — The Discovery

A natural introduction, not a product shot

"Then, in a desperate moment, I found MarsMen. I wasn't expecting everything to change overnight. I just wanted something that could support what I was already doing."
MarsMen enters naturally into his morning routine.
Because the audience has already seen problem → frustration → attempts to solve it, MarsMen becomes a discovery rather than an advertisement interruption.
Scene 04 — The Change

The drive comes back — not a new person

Before: low energy, slower movement, muted environment
After: more energy, faster movement, brighter environment
"I didn't want to become someone else. I just wanted that drive I had when I first started."
The payoff isn't "MarsMen magically changed me" — it's "I found the drive I was looking for," connecting the product to the emotional motivation without an unsupported guarantee.
Scene 05 — CTA

Closing the loop from Scene 01

"If you're looking for that drive again, see what MarsMen is about."
The athlete finishes his routine and prepares to train as the music peaks.
"I don't feel the same anymore" (Scene 01) is answered by "looking for that drive again?" — so the CTA feels connected to the story rather than a generic "Buy Now."
