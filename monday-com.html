A research-driven teardown of monday.com's positioning, messaging, and ad creative, built the way a real diagnosis should be: graded by how solid the evidence actually is, not just how confident it sounds.

PREPARED SEP 04, 2026 SUBJECT: MONDAY.COM (NASDAQ: MNDY) ANALYST: JOLLY DANIEL TENORIO

How to read this report

Every claim below carries a label showing how solid the evidence behind it is. This is competitive intelligence built entirely from publicly available and third party sources. I have never had access to monday.com's ad account, analytics, or internal numbers, and nothing here should be read as if I did.

Meta blocks automated access to its own Ad Library, so some ad level detail could not be pulled directly: exact impressions per ad, and the precise split between partnership and brand ads. Rather than invent numbers to fill those gaps, they are marked Not available, with the manual method to collect them in the final section.

Target Audience

Reconstructed from monday.com's own segment messaging, product lineup, and case-study mix, not from individual ad-targeting data, which advertisers don't disclose publicly. My interpretation

Persona 01

The Overwhelmed Operator

SMB or mid-market ops/PMO lead drowning in spreadsheets and Slack threads, not a specific tool. Wants visibility without a technical build.

  • Drawn to templates (200+) that shortcut setup
  • Real signal: monday.com reports SMB users running 3.5x more AI actions than enterprise users

The Cross-Functional Marketing Lead

Runs campaigns across channels and stakeholders; needs shared visibility more than raw task tracking.

IT/ops decision-maker at a larger org evaluating how to roll out AI agents without losing control or compliance.

03

Offer

Per-seat, per-product, with AI now sold as a metered add-on across every tier. Observed, pulled live from monday.com/pricing.

Per-seat monthly price, billed annually, entry paid tier vs. "Pro" tier, across the four core products. Enterprise is custom-quoted on all four and excluded here.

What's actually in the offer

Free plan (2 seats, 3 boards) and a 14-day Pro trial with no credit card. Every paid tier now bundles an "AI credits" allowance (Sidekick assistant, AI agent workforce, Vibe app builder). AI isn't a separate SKU, it's metered consumption stacked on top of seat price. 18% discount for annual billing; discounted nonprofit pricing available.

Proof the offer converts

McDonald's: 40% faster production time. Canva: 25% shorter project timelines, ~1,224 hours/month saved. VML: $250K saved yearly. One customer reported 105K hours saved annually; another, 517% growth in annual accounts. All from monday.com's own published case studies.

04

Positioning

monday.com's own comparison page names its real competitive set. Where each one sits is my read, not a scored ranking. My interpretation

monday.com names Asana, Trello, Smartsheet, Airtable, Wrike, Basecamp, Jira, and Workfront as its comparison set. Observed It positions itself as the most horizontal, least technical option of the group: "the most holistic work management solution for any type of team, fully customizable to their preferred way of working." That's a deliberate trade: broad and visual over deep and specialized in any one function.

The bigger positioning move is the one happening right now, the April 2026 shift from "Work Management Platform" to "AI Work Platform." That's not a repositioning against Asana or Jira anymore; it's a bid to be compared against AI-native tools, with "agents working alongside people" as the new frame. It's brand new, which means it's still ownable if they keep repeating it, and vulnerable if they don't.

05

Messaging

Five patterns repeat across four years of campaigns. Observed, each tied to a dated, named campaign.

Spacing is roughly proportional to elapsed time. Note the gap: quiet from 2022–2023, then a sharp acceleration in campaign and positioning cadence from mid-2024 onward.

1. Make B2B software feel emotionally warm

Singing llamas, a llama-farm dashboard easter egg since 2019, "Love to Use" as a campaign name, an unusual amount of whimsy for project-management software.

2. Buy attention at consumer scale

A Super Bowl spot and Vogue's first-ever software brand partnership (Met Gala) are consumer-marketing moves, not typical B2B SaaS plays. They sell category confidence more than any one feature.

3. Flexibility is the one constant hero

"Work without limits," "for whatever you run," "fully customizable." Every era of messaging returns to the same core wedge: no-code customization for any team.

4. Every proof point is a number

Case studies are never just "customers love it". It's always 25%, 40%, $250K, 517%. A disciplined, consistent proof format.

5. (2026, and accelerating) AI as a teammate, not a feature

"Sidekick," "digital worker," "humans and agents working together," AI agents that can literally sign up and log in as platform users. AI is being framed as a new category of user, not a bullet point on a features page. This is the newest pattern and the one worth watching most closely.

Creative Strategy

The ad level analysis. Some of this is solid public data and some of it hit a hard access wall. Both are marked plainly.

Format breakdown Estimated

A third-party ad-tracking tool (Opus, querying Meta's Ad Library via the ScrapeCreators API) had a snapshot from April 30, 2026 showing 50 active monday.com creatives, all classified as Carousel format. I'd treat the "100% Carousel" figure with real skepticism. It's one tool's read, four months stale, and out of step with monday.com's known heavy investment in video (Super Bowl spot, singing-llama campaign, Singapore 360° film). The likely real picture is a two-tier system: big-budget hero video for brand campaigns, plus always-on carousel/static for everyday Meta demand-gen, but I can't confirm that split with confidence from what's accessible here.

50 active creatives tracked as of the April 30, 2026 snapshot. Source: opus.pro ad-library tracker, citing Meta Ad Library via ScrapeCreators API.

Partnership vs. brand ad ratio Not available

I could not pull this. Meta's own Ad Library site disallows automated fetching (its robots.txt blocks it outright), and no accessible mirror gave me a per-ad list with visible creator handles. What I can confirm: monday.com has a real, ongoing influencer-marketing practice, a 2020–21 campaign with TopRank Marketing generated 17.9M in potential reach from remote-work-expert partnerships, and the company currently has an open "Influencer Marketing Manager" role covering Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram, X, and TikTok. So the practice exists; the current on-Meta ratio of "with @creator" posts to straight brand ads is a real, gettable number, just not one I could get automatically. Section 11 has the exact 10-minute manual method.

Top 10 ads by impressions Not available

This is now genuinely possible to get, more possible than it's ever been. As of January 2026, Meta's Ad Library started publishing impression-range buckets (under 1K, 1K–5K, 5K–10K, 10K–50K, 50K–100K, 100K–500K, 500K–1M, 1M+) for every commercial ad, not just political ones, plus a "Low impression count" badge under 100. That's a real, recent, and useful upgrade. But the library itself is a JavaScript-rendered tool that blocks automated access, so getting an actual ranked top 10 means a human sorting it live, about 10 minutes of work, walked through in Section 11. A fabricated top ten, with invented ad copy and made up numbers, would make every other finding in this report worth less. So it is marked as a gap instead.

Longest-running ads & trend summary Estimated

Per the same April 2026 snapshot, the single longest-running active creative had been live 79 days against a 71-day average, a narrow spread, which reads as a fairly disciplined account (not a huge pile of long-dead zombie ads still spending). The standard read on "longest running = likely winner" still applies as a heuristic, with the caveat that cost-cap/bid-cap buying can leave losers live longer than they deserve, so runtime alone shouldn't be the only signal. Pair it with the impression-range bucket once you pull it yourself.

Creative velocity Observed (campaign level) / Not available (day-to-day)

At the brand-campaign level, the cadence is verifiable and telling: roughly one major campaign every 12–18 months from 2022–2023, then four distinct campaigns/positioning moves in the 18 months from mid-2024 to late-2025 (see the timeline in Section 05). That acceleration is real. Day-to-day performance ad upload cadence (how many new Meta variants per week, and the exact date of the most recent upload) needs the live library; I don't have a reliable way to state a "last upload was on X date" without fabricating it.

07

Customer Journey

A textbook product-led-growth funnel, with a parallel sales-assisted lane for anything over ~40 seats. Observed / My interpretation on the funnel framing.

Free entry, zero friction

Free plan (2 seats) or a 14-day Pro trial, no credit card required. 200+ templates shortcut time-to-first-value.

In-product AI metering nudges upgrade

AI credits are attached to every paid tier and consumed by normal use, "need more? add credits anytime" is a built-in upgrade trigger, not just a sales pitch.

Cross-sell across the suite

Work Management customers get funneled toward CRM, Dev, Campaigns, and Service, a shared seat/billing infrastructure makes each one an easy add, not a new vendor decision.

Enterprise breaks off into "Contact sales"

Past ~40 seats, self-serve signup disappears in favor of a quote request, dedicated onboarding, and a named Customer Success Manager.

Customer Research

Real, dated reviewer language, not paraphrased marketing copy. Observed, pulled from G2 reviews (via AWS Marketplace mirror) and SoftwareFinder.

Where the friction is

"Even the most basic plan with no guest access costs $8/month", pricing is the single most repeated complaint across years of reviews.

"When using it for the first time, the abundance of features can be overwhelming, necessitating a significant learning curve."

That last point tracks with something I ran into directly while researching this report: "Monday" also names an unrelated Australian haircare brand with its own large influencer program. Search-and-brand confusion isn't a hypothetical here. I had to actively filter it out of these findings.

10

Weaknesses & Opportunities

Synthesized from everything above. My interpretation

Weaknesses

Pricing is the #1 recurring complaint

Per-seat cost, a 3-seat minimum on paid tiers, and steep jumps between tiers show up in reviews spanning 2022 through 2023, a durable, not one-off, friction point.

A genuinely collision-prone brand name

"Monday" is generic enough to share search real estate with unrelated brands (a haircare company among them), a real SEO and brand-recall tax that a more distinctive name wouldn't carry.

Feature sprawl creates its own onboarding tax

The flexibility that's the core sell doubles as the most-cited learning curve complaint, reviewers repeatedly note "overwhelming" feature depth at first use.

Opportunities

The "AI Work Platform" repositioning is brand new

Announced April 2026, the message is still fresh enough to be ownable if repeated consistently before a competitor claims the same territory.

A deep case-study bank is underused as performance creative

Specific stats (25% faster, $250K saved, 517% growth) are proven ad material, but the visible creative supply (per the proxy data in Section 06) still leans generic-carousel rather than stat-led direct response.

The llama is a genuinely ownable, low-cost creative asset

Recurring since 2019 and still deployed in 2025 campaigns, most B2B SaaS competitors have nothing this distinctive, and it's cheap to keep reusing in always-on creative, not just hero spots.

Bonus: Data You CAN Collect

The exact, free, no-login methods to close the three "Data gap" items above yourself, reusable on any future brand teardown.

Pull monday.com's live Meta ad set (~10 minutes)

Open the Ad Library

Go to facebook.com/ads/library in any browser, no login needed. Set Country to your target market (or "All"), and Ad category to "All ads."

Search the exact Page name

Search "monday.com." Confirm you're on their verified Page, not a lookalike or reseller.

Read the impression-range bucket on each ad

Since January 2026, every commercial ad shows a bucket (under 1K, 1K to 5K, 5K to 10K, 10K to 50K, 50K to 100K, 100K to 500K, 500K to 1M, or 1M+) plus a "Low impression count" badge under 100. Sort your own shortlist by this before runtime; delivery is a stronger signal than age.

Scan ad copy for "with @" to tag partnership vs. brand

Open each ad's text and creator tags. Anything featuring a creator handle or "with [Name]" is a partnership ad; tally the two buckets yourself for the real current ratio.

Note start dates for velocity and last-upload

Each ad shows "Started running on [date]." The most recent start date across the set is your real "last upload", sort newest-first to find it directly.

Other free, no-login transparency tools

If you want more than the free tools give you

Foreplay, BigSpy, and PowerAdSpy are paid ad-intelligence platforms that add spend estimates, cross-account replication tracking, and bulk export on top of what the free libraries show. None were needed for the findings above, but they're the natural next step if you're doing this kind of teardown regularly.
