The Purest Co.

DTC Supplements · Competitor research and creative concept

Competitor research across positioning, messaging, and customer journey, built around one tension: trust versus skepticism in a crowded supplements category.

What I found

Social proof clusters around established hero products, while newer SKUs carry far less review volume. Transparency does the heavy lifting everywhere else.

What I proposed

A UGC concept that opens on the customer's skepticism instead of the product, with a full scene by scene script.

The insight it turned on The customer is not looking for a supplement. They are looking for a reason to trust one. So skepticism becomes the hook rather than the obstacle.

MM

MarsMen

DTC Men's Health · Research, strategy, and creative concept

Two observations about their existing marketing, a reworked creative strategy, and a five scene concept built to test against what they were already running.

What I found

A five capsule serving creates real friction for a first time buyer. Separately, their existing creator relationships were being used for reach rather than as a repeatable creative system.

What I proposed

"I Want My Drive Back," a creator led concept where the athlete experiences the problem instead of endorsing the product.

The structural change Their current ads run athlete, then product, then brand message. The proposed structure runs problem, then the athlete's experience of it, then the product. The audience gets a reason to care before anything is sold.

Hey %FIRSTNAME%,

You're officially in.

Over the next 7 days, I'll walk you through the roadmap to creating content that gets noticed.

Day 1: MISTAKES
Discover why your videos aren't getting the views you want. By identifying what's holding your content back, you'll know what to fix instead of guessing.

Day 4: ACTION PLAN
Put what you've learned into practice with a clear plan for improving your content.

UPGRADE TO VIP
In-depth training · 1-on-1 coaching call · Priority Q&A · Additional strategies

Why it's built this way: the roadmap does the retention work. Naming all four days up front gives a reason to open the next email, and the VIP offer sits after the value so it reads as an upgrade rather than a pitch.
Email 2 · Value and objection
More Videos Doesn't Mean More Views
Preview: Those are just videos. Here's what actually matters.

Hey %FIRSTNAME%,

More videos means more views, right?

Wrong.

Posting more content doesn't guarantee that anyone will watch it.

If I were starting from scratch, I'd focus on three things first:

1. Know Your Niche
Before creating more content, understand who you're creating it for. If you don't know your audience, it's difficult to create something they'll actually care about.

2. Master Your Hooks
Your hook determines whether someone stops scrolling, or keeps going. A great video can still fail if the first few seconds don't give people a reason to watch.

3. Understand What Keeps People Watching
Getting the click, or the initial view, isn't enough. You need to understand what makes viewers stay, watch, and come back for more.

And that's exactly what I teach inside my coaching program.

I've helped creators grow to a combined 49K+ students, and I've built my coaching around helping creators understand what actually drives content performance.

Don't just take my word for it.

GO VIRAL →

Why it's built this way: it opens by stating the reader's own assumption back to them, then breaks it in one word. The three teaching points are real value, so the bridge into the offer is earned rather than bolted on.

The Chair Won't Stay Empty
